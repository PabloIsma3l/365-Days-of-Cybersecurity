## 🧠 Why Alert Reporting Matters

Accurate reporting ensures:

* Efficient escalation to L2/L3 analysts
* Clear visibility of risk and impact
* Proper tracking of incidents
* Legal and compliance documentation
* Business awareness of security threats

Poor reporting can delay response and increase risk exposure.

---

## 🏢 Responsibilities of a SOC L1 Analyst in Reporting

A Level 1 SOC Analyst must:

* Document investigation findings
* Provide supporting evidence
* Classify severity correctly
* Escalate confirmed threats
* Maintain professional and concise communication

---

## 📝 Structure of a Proper SOC Alert Report

A high-quality report typically includes:

### 1️⃣ Executive Summary

* Brief description of the alert
* Affected asset(s)
* Severity level

### 2️⃣ Alert Details

* Detection rule triggered
* Date and time (UTC)
* Source and destination IP
* Username involved (if applicable)

### 3️⃣ Investigation Steps

* Logs reviewed
* Tools used (SIEM, EDR, etc.)
* Correlated alerts
* Threat intelligence checks

### 4️⃣ Findings

* Evidence of malicious activity (if any)
* Indicators of Compromise (IOCs)
* Impact assessment

### 5️⃣ Final Classification

* False Positive
* True Positive (Low / Medium / High)

### 6️⃣ Escalation Details

* Escalated to (L2 / IR team / Engineering)
* Recommended actions

---

## 🚨 When to Escalate

Escalation is required when:

* Malicious activity is confirmed
* Privileged accounts are compromised
* Lateral movement is detected
* Data exfiltration is suspected
* Critical systems are impacted

Timely escalation reduces dwell time.

---

## 💬 Communication Best Practices

* Be concise and factual
* Avoid assumptions
* Use clear severity labels
* Include timestamps and evidence
* Maintain professional tone

Good communication is as important as technical skills in a SOC environment.

---

## 🧰 Tools Supporting Reporting

* SIEM dashboards
* EDR consoles

* Threat intelligence platforms
* Internal knowledge bases
