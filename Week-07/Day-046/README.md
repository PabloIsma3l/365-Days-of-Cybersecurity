## 🧠 What is Incident Response?

Incident Response (IR) is a structured approach used to detect, manage, and mitigate cybersecurity incidents.

A security incident may include:

* Unauthorized access
* Malware infections
* Data breaches
* Privilege escalation
* Insider threats

The goal of IR is to minimize impact, reduce recovery time, and prevent recurrence.

---

## 🔄 Incident Response Lifecycle

The standard IR process follows these phases:

### 1️⃣ Preparation

* Define policies and procedures
* Establish communication plans
* Deploy monitoring and detection tools
* Conduct training and simulations

---

### 2️⃣ Identification

* Detect suspicious activity
* Validate alerts
* Determine scope and impact
* Classify incident severity

---

### 3️⃣ Containment

Short-term containment:

* Isolate affected systems
* Disable compromised accounts

Long-term containment:

* Apply patches
* Implement temporary fixes

---

### 4️⃣ Eradication

* Remove malware
* Eliminate attacker persistence
* Patch vulnerabilities

---

### 5️⃣ Recovery

* Restore systems from backups
* Monitor for reinfection
* Validate system integrity

---

### 6️⃣ Lessons Learned

* Document findings
* Identify gaps in detection
* Improve procedures
* Update security controls

---

## 👥 Incident Response Roles

Typical IR team roles:

* SOC Analyst (Detection & Triage)
* Incident Responder
* Threat Intelligence Analyst
* Forensic Analyst
* Management / Communication Lead

Clear communication and defined responsibilities are critical.

---

## 🚨 Types of Security Incidents

Common incident categories include:

* Malware infection (ransomware, trojans, spyware)
* Phishing attacks
* Data breaches
* Privilege escalation
* Insider threats
* Denial of Service (DoS/DDoS)
* Web application attacks (SQLi, XSS)

Understanding the type of incident helps determine containment strategy and response priority.

---

## 🛡️ Security Solutions Used in Incident Response

Organizations rely on multiple security technologies to detect and respond to incidents:

### 📊 SIEM (Security Information and Event Management)

* Centralized log collection
* Event correlation
* Alert generation
* Threat detection visibility

---

### 🛑 AV (Antivirus)

* Signature-based malware detection
* Basic real-time protection
* File quarantine capabilities

---

### 🧠 EDR (Endpoint Detection & Response)

* Behavioral detection
* Process monitoring
* Endpoint isolation
* Threat hunting capability

EDR provides deeper visibility than traditional antivirus.

---

### 🔥 IDS/IPS

* Network traffic monitoring
* Intrusion detection
* Active blocking (IPS)

---

### 🧩 SOAR (Security Orchestration, Automation & Response)

* Automates response workflows
* Integrates with SIEM & EDR
* Reduces response time

---

## 📊 Documentation & Reporting

Proper documentation includes:

* Timeline of events
* Indicators of Compromise (IOCs)
* Affected systems
* Evidence collected
* Actions taken

Accurate reporting ensures accountability and legal defensibility.

---

## 🔐 Indicators of Compromise (IOCs)

Examples of IOCs:

* Malicious IP addresses
* Suspicious domain names
* File hashes
* Unusual login activity
* Unknown processes

IOCs help identify scope and support threat hunting.

## 📚 Frameworks: NIST vs SANS

Understanding major cybersecurity frameworks is essential in Incident Response.

### 🏛️ NIST (National Institute of Standards and Technology)

NIST provides standardized cybersecurity frameworks widely adopted by organizations worldwide.

Key reference:

* NIST Incident Response Lifecycle (Preparation, Identification, Containment, Eradication, Recovery, Lessons Learned)

Focus:

* Policy-driven
* Compliance-oriented
* Structured governance model

Used heavily in:

* Government environments
* Enterprise compliance programs
* Risk management strategies

---

### 🎓 SANS Institute

SANS provides practical cybersecurity training and operational guidance.

SANS Incident Response model typically includes:

* Preparation
* Identification
* Containment
* Eradication
* Recovery
* Lessons Learned

Focus:

* Hands-on implementation
* Tactical response techniques
* Real-world attack scenarios

---

### 🔎 Key Difference

* **NIST** → Strategic framework & compliance-driven guidance
* **SANS** → Practical, operational security training & methodologies

Both complement each other in professional IR environments.
